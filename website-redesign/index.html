Does your business website need a redesign?

Eight signs worth checking, how to check each one yourself in a few minutes with free tools, and what to fix before you pay anyone — me included — to rebuild the whole thing.

About 7 minutes to read By Keshav Prashar, freelance React & MERN developer, Jaipur Last updated 8 October 2026

The short answer

A website needs a redesign when it stops doing its one job — turning the people who find you into calls, WhatsApp messages and enquiries — and small fixes can no longer get it back. If only one or two of the signs below apply, you may need a repair, not a rebuild. If four or more apply, a redesign is usually cheaper than patching.

Eight signs, and how to check each one

1. It is awkward to use on a phone

Most visitors to a small business site arrive on a phone. Open your site on yours and try to find your phone number, your prices and your location with one thumb. Text you have to pinch to read, buttons too small to tap, or a menu that hides everything are the signs.

2. It loads slowly on mobile data

Paste your address into PageSpeed Insights and look at the mobile result. Google treats a Largest Contentful Paint of 2.5 seconds or less as good; well beyond that, people wait on a blank or half-drawn page, and some of them go back to the search results instead.

3. Nobody can contact you in one tap

Check three things: a phone number that dials when tapped, a WhatsApp button, and a contact form that actually delivers. Send yourself a test enquiry through your own form. A form that silently fails is the most expensive bug a business website can have, because you never hear about the leads you lost.

4. The browser says "Not secure"

Look at the address bar. If there is no padlock, or the browser warns that the site is not secure, the site is missing HTTPS. Visitors notice, and forms on that page send what people type without encryption.

5. Google cannot find your pages

Search site:yourdomain.com on Google. If only a page or two appear, or pages you deleted years ago still do, search engines are not seeing the site you think you have. Google Search Console will tell you exactly which pages are indexed and why the rest are not.

6. The information is out of date

Old prices, services you no longer offer, a team that has changed, a copyright year from several years back. Each one is small; together they tell a visitor nobody is looking after the business.

7. You cannot change it without a developer you can no longer reach

If a one-line text change means finding whoever built the site, the site is a liability. A redesign is the moment to make sure you own the code, the domain and the hosting account, and that routine edits are either easy for you or quick for someone else.

8. The first screen does not say what you do

Show your homepage to someone for five seconds, then ask what the business does, who it is for and how to contact it. If they cannot answer all three, the most important part of the site is not working.

Before you pay for a full rebuild Several of these are repairs, not redesigns: adding a WhatsApp button, fixing a broken form, compressing images, turning on HTTPS or updating the text. If those are the only problems, ask for them as fixes. I will tell you when that is the honest answer.

Two causes accounted for most of the difference: the headline area stayed invisible until a script finished running, and the font was loaded from a third-party stylesheet that blocked the first paint. Neither is visible by looking at a site; both show up in a five-minute test. Lab scores vary from run to run, so treat the numbers as a direction, not a guarantee.

What a redesign should include

  • Your content carried across, rewritten only where it is unclear — not thrown away.
  • Your existing addresses kept wherever possible, with redirects for the ones that change, so search rankings you already have are not reset.
  • A layout tested on real phone widths, from a small 360px screen up to a wide desktop.
  • A contact route that works: tap-to-call, WhatsApp and a form that is tested end to end.
  • The basics search engines read: page titles, descriptions, headings and a sitemap.
  • Handover: the code, and the domain and hosting in your name.

What a redesign costs
